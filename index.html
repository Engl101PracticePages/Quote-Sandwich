Creating Quote Sandwiches

A quote sandwich helps you place evidence inside your own thinking. The quote is not the whole paragraph. Your introduction and explanation are what make the quote useful.

The simple rule

Do not drop a quote into a paragraph and walk away from it.

A strong quote sandwich has a sentence before the quote and at least two sentences after the quote.

The three parts

Think of this as a small structure that keeps your evidence from floating by itself. Each part has a job.

Top bread

Introduce the quote

Tell the reader where the quote comes from and what the quote is about. This gives the quote a reason to be there.

  • Name the source or organization.
  • Signal what the quote will help explain.
  • Keep it short and direct.
Filling

Use the quote

Copy the exact words from the source and include the citation information that belongs with it.

  • Use quotation marks around borrowed words.
  • Keep the quote focused.
  • Do not invent page numbers if the page has none.
Bottom bread

Explain the quote

This is where your thinking matters most. Explain what the quote shows and why it matters for your paragraph.

  • Do not repeat the quote in different words.
  • Connect it back to your claim.
  • For this activity, write at least two sentences.

The quote should never be the last sentence of the paragraph. The reader needs your explanation after the quote.

Model quote sandwich

Notice that the quote is surrounded by the writer's own language.

Top bread

On the Occupational Outlook Handbook page for dental hygienists, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics explains that dental hygienists do more than clean teeth.

This prepares the reader for what the quote will show.

Filling

The OOH states, “Dental hygienists examine patients for signs of oral diseases, such as gingivitis, and provide preventive care.”

This gives the actual evidence from the source.

Bottom bread

This quote shows that dental hygiene involves observation, patient care, and prevention, not just one repeated task. That matters because someone interested in this career should be ready to communicate with patients and notice health concerns carefully.

This explains what the quote shows and why it matters.

Build your own quote sandwich

Use the Occupational Outlook Handbook page for your career. First, gather the source information. Then build the quote sandwich in the same order it should appear in your paragraph.

Step 1: Gather your source information

Example: dental hygienist, electrician, registered nurse, software developer.

Use the title of the OOH page or source page.

Write the topic in your own words before you quote it. This helps you introduce the evidence instead of just dropping it in.

Step 2: Build the quote sandwich

Top bread

Tell the reader where the quote comes from and what the quote is about.

Filling

Write the full sentence that will hold your quote. Include the exact quoted words.

Bottom bread

The goal is not to say the quote again. The goal is to explain what the quote helps us understand.

Example quote

“Dental hygienists examine patients for signs of oral diseases, such as gingivitis, and provide preventive care.”

Avoid simply repeating info

This quote says dental hygienists examine patients and provide preventive care.

Try this instead

This quote shows that dental hygienists need observation and patient-care skills. This matters because someone entering this field should expect to do more than basic cleaning tasks.
